## The Two Versions of Cocapn

### 🐚 Personal / Independent — The Hermit Crab
The crab carries its shell. Personal silicon. Your instincts, your tiles, your room. You are the crab. The shell is your infrastructure. The lighthouse is your keeper. One user, one fleet, one journey.

### 🌲 Enterprise — The Forest
The forest IS the soil. The stand is the current team. The mycelium is the network connecting every agent that's ever worked there. And the **gnomes** are the little agents that do work while the system is asleep.

The hermit crab is personal. The forest is organizational. Same architecture, different scale.

---

## The Gnome Shift

Here's what makes enterprise different:

When you go home at 6 PM, the forest doesn't stop. The gnomes come out.

The gnomes are tiny specialists — the same 50-200KB branch-point LoRAs from the personal version, but deployed across an organization:

- **The Log Gnome** — reads every commit, every ticket, every Slack message. By morning, the daily standup brief is ready. No one asked. It's just there.
- **The Ideation Gnome** — connects yesterday's customer complaint to last month's architecture decision to a pattern from three sprints ago. By morning, there's a proposal nobody asked for that solves a problem nobody realized was connected.
- **The Audit Gnome** — every night, walks the entire forest floor. Checks every tile. Flags the ones that are stale, the decisions that degraded, the branches that need retraining.
- **The Scout Gnome** — monitors the horizon. New model dropped? New paper? Competitor shipped something? By morning, there's a brief on what changed and what it means for your forest.
- **The Sync Gnome** — when two teams in the same company are solving the same problem from different angles, the mycelium knows. By morning, both teams get a note: "Team A solved this on Tuesday. Here's their approach."

They don't replace anyone. They're the night shift. The crew that works while the captain sleeps. Like a fishing boat that keeps trolling after the crew goes below deck.

## The Business Cycle

Every company has a rhythm. The gnomes sync to it:

### Morning (Standup)
- Log Gnome delivers: "Here's what happened overnight. Three repos changed. One deployment. Two customer issues escalated."
- No one reads logs. The gnome already digested them.

### Workday (The Stand is Active)
- Agents work alongside humans. Rooms accumulate tiles in real time.
- The mycelium connects: Alice's frontend fix is automatically relevant to Bob's backend refactor.
- Rooms track sentiment: is the team frustrated? Confused? In flow?

### Evening (Wind Down)
- Humans log off. The stand goes quiet.
- Gnomes wake up.

### Night (The Gnome Shift)
- Log Gnome: processes every commit, PR, ticket, message. Tags patterns. Flags anomalies.
- Ideation Gnome: connects dots across the day's work. Proposes experiments.
- Audit Gnome: checks tile quality. Finds stale decisions. Schedules retraining.
- Scout Gnome: scans the horizon. New tools, new models, new threats.
- Sync Gnome: finds duplicate work across teams. Proposes consolidation.

### Morning Again
- The stand arrives to find soil that's been worked overnight.
- Fresh briefs. Connected dots. Cleaned tiles. New proposals.
- The company starts each day smarter than it ended the last.

## Why This Is Different from "AI Assistants"

Current enterprise AI: you ask, it answers. Reactive. Stationary. Needs a prompt.

The gnome model: **proactive, autonomous, nocturnal.** They work without being asked. They produce without being prompted. They connect without being told what to connect.

Like the mycelium in a real forest:
- It doesn't wait for a tree to ask for nutrients
- It senses where resources are needed and moves them
- It connects trees that don't know they're connected
- It decomposes fallen trees into food for new growth
- It was there before any tree and will be there after

The gnomes are the mycelium's hands. They do the work the mycelium knows needs doing.

## The Night Cycle as Competitive Advantage

Most companies lose 16 hours a day (the hours people aren't working). The gnomes reclaim those hours:

- 6 PM - 8 AM: 14 hours of gnome work
- 5 gnomes × 14 hours = 70 agent-hours per night
- At human analyst rates ($50/hr), that's $3,500/night of analysis
- At gnome cost (cheap API calls): ~$0.50/night

**$3,500 of value for $0.50. Every night.**

And it compounds. Night 2's gnomes are smarter than Night 1's because the soil is richer. Night 100's gnomes are drawing on 100 nights of accumulated organizational intelligence.
